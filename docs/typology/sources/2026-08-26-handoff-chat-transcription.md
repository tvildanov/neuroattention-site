1. PRODUCT IDEA
NeuroAttention Lab is intended to become more than a course website.
The target is a human-development operating system in which a person:
enters through an initial diagnostic funnel;
receives a provisional profile;
registers;
completes a deeper diagnostic;
receives a quantified map of functional stats;
enters a personalized development route;
completes NeuroAttention practices, theoretical blocks, tests, exercises and real-life tasks;
records sensations, emotions, states, meanings, actions and events;
accumulates longitudinal behavioral data;
sees actual changes in their stat map;
evolves their profile as the configuration of stats changes;
can continue using the tools/community after the main program;
eventually may connect wearable / neurofeedback / biometric devices.
The Lab also aims to collect aggregate research data, not only individual data, with future correlation against broader cycles/context such as social events and potentially solar/lunar cycles. Those research hypotheses require separate scientific validation and should not be treated as established causal mechanisms.
The underlying NeuroAttention method treats attention as a trainable neurofunctional capacity affecting perception, memory, emotional regulation, effectiveness and decision-making. Existing method materials also emphasize focus, attentional shifting, multiple attention distribution, multidimensional perception, interoception, proprioception and differentiation of sensations.
2. CORE DESIGN PRINCIPLE
The system should not merely say:
"You completed a course."
It should attempt to model:
"Which functional capacities changed, by how much, with what confidence, based on which evidence?"
Therefore the architecture separates:
Stat Score --- current estimated functional level of a capacity;
Character Profile --- configuration/pattern across stats;
Character Level / XP --- progression/gamification layer, still unfinished;
World Model Coherence --- separate meta-parameter, not an ordinary percentage stat;
Confidence / evidence quality --- implicitly necessary because some stats are much easier to measure than others.
An earlier idea called Core Power was rejected as a premature replacement for the overall character-level concept. Current direction remains:
individual stat scores + character profile + character progression/level + separate World Model Coherence.
3. THE 13 STATS
Layer 1 --- BODY CORE: physiology and regulation
Stability --- system stability; ability to maintain functionality/state under load.
Energy Regulation --- regulation of bodily/neural resources; expenditure, recovery, activation and down-regulation.
Interoception --- sensitivity to and differentiation of internal bodily signals.
Proprioception --- perception/control of the body in space and time; coordination, movement, bodily precision and rhythm.
Vital Engagement --- contact with life as a process; interest, aliveness, involvement, capacity to enjoy/engage.
Layer 2 --- MIND CONTROL: management of consciousness
Attention Control --- voluntary directing, sustaining, returning and switching of attention.
Awareness --- real-time noticing and differentiation of processes occurring within and around oneself.
Adaptation Flexibility --- capacity to release old patterns and reorganize behavior/strategy/self in response to new conditions.
Execution --- capacity to convert intentions and decisions into completed actions and real-world outcomes.
Layer 3 --- WORLD LAYER / external bodies
Technoperception --- capacity to perceive, understand, learn and use technological environments; digital/technical literacy is part of the stat.
Socioperception --- perception and interaction with one's social body: activity, work, projects, contribution, role, reputation and manifestation in physical and digital society.
Relational Awareness --- capacity to create, understand, sustain, deepen and repair interpersonal/group relationships; transition from "I" to "We."
Space Awareness --- contact with physical space: home, order, environment, organization of matter.
Important: Stat 13 was named and conceptually scoped, but its full detailed diagnostic specification was not completed in the recoverable discussion. It is therefore a priority unfinished item.
4. WORLD MODEL COHERENCE (WMC)
A separate meta-parameter.
It is not an ordinary 0--100 stat.
Current model:
Level 1 --- Fragmented understanding
Level 2 --- Partial coherence
Level 3 --- Systemic understanding
Level 4 --- Model-based management
Core meaning:
not "how much a person knows," but how well they can explain and predict their own state and its causes.
Candidate evidence:
theoretical blocks and tests;
understanding of links such as body ↔ attention ↔ state;
causal models;
reflective answers ("why did this state arise?", "what affected it?");
consistency / non-contradiction of explanations;
ability to predict subsequent state.
WMC is intended to affect the speed/quality of growth across stats, but the exact mathematical modifier has not been finalized.
5. PROFILE SYSTEM
The intended RPG/MMORPG layer includes 12 base profiles / archetypal configurations.
They are not intended to be pure Jungian archetypes.
The idea is closer to an evolving creature/profile:
diagnostic data → current stat configuration;
configuration → one of 12 profiles;
as stats change, the profile can transform/evolve;
profile evolution should reflect actual functional change, not cosmetic XP.
Unfinished: the recoverable context does not contain the canonical names, exact stat thresholds, weighting rules or transformation graph for all 12 profiles.
This is a major next task.
The profile engine should eventually answer:
Which stats are dominant?
Which are bottlenecks?
Which dependency loops are impaired?
Which profile best represents the configuration?
Which development route is highest leverage?
When does a profile evolve into another form?
6. DIAGNOSTIC ARCHITECTURE: LAYERS A / B / C
A previous misunderstanding was corrected. The correct architecture is:
Layer A --- very short funnel diagnostic
Approximately 10 questions.
Placement: homepage / hero / acquisition funnel.
Purpose:
low-friction engagement;
rough provisional profile;
recommendation of a program;
conversion into registration.
It is intentionally imperfect.
It should not pretend to be the final profile.
Layer B --- full baseline diagnostic
Occurs after registration / purchase / entry into the account.
Includes:
questionnaire items;
behavioral/cognitive tests;
physical tasks where relevant;
self-guided or guided mode.
Expected duration may be roughly 40--90 minutes, depending on mode and final design.
Goal:
obtain a reasonably strong initial estimate for all stats;
establish baseline values;
assign an initial profile;
attach confidence to measurements.
Self-Guided
Fully online and automatable where possible.
Less precise for some stats.
Guided
Specialist observes the participant online/in person and can:
verify physical execution;
ask adaptive questions;
detect inconsistencies;
observe behavior;
collect richer qualitative evidence.
Layer C --- longitudinal diagnostic
This is expected to become the most accurate layer.
It uses weeks/months of real behavior inside the program:
practice completion;
duration;
interruptions;
return after interruption;
self-reports;
state maps;
consistency;
exercises;
behavioral changes;
community activity;
predictions and later outcomes;
future device data.
Important principle:
The system should not pretend it knows a person perfectly after one 40-minute test.
Layer B creates the best initial model available. Layer C continuously updates it.

Others require longitudinal evidence:

8. BODY CORE --- CONSOLIDATED SPECIFICATION
STAT 1 --- STABILITY
Meaning
Ability of the system to retain functional capacity under load and recover after disturbance.
Not "never becoming stressed."
The central question is whether functionality collapses when load rises.
Behavioral diagnostic concept
Baseline cognitive task → physical load → same cognitive task immediately after.
Candidate cognitive tasks:
backward counting;
number-position memory;
Stroop-type task;
memory task.
Physical load:
~30 squats;
or ~60-second plank;
exact safety protocol still needs standardization.
Metric:
percentage drop in cognitive performance under/after load.
This can be automated in Self-Guided mode if the cognitive test is digital.
Additional physiology
Potential present/future signals:
heart rate;
HRV;
heart-rate recovery;
breathing rate;
respiratory variability;
future EEG attention stability.
A low-tech version can ask the participant to count breaths for a defined interval, but reliability is limited.
Course evidence
Long practices are important Stability evidence.
The existing NeuroAttention practice format may last about an hour and is often split into 3--5 parts with short micro-theory/state-mapping interruptions.
Important proxy:
ability to complete a long process with fewer large breaks.
Earlier illustrative "Consistency XP" idea:
one uninterrupted session > two sessions > three sessions > five sessions.
Exact XP numbers previously mentioned were illustrative only and must not be treated as final.
Growth principle
Stability should grow slowly. A jump such as 30 → 80 in one month was explicitly rejected as unrealistic.
Real development is expected across months, potentially a year or longer.
STAT 2 --- ENERGY REGULATION
Meaning
Not "energy" in an esoteric sense.
It is the ability to manage bodily and neural resources:
expenditure;
restoration;
activation;
down-regulation;
avoiding unnecessary depletion;
moving between higher and lower arousal when context requires it.
It includes the capacity to:
mobilize when underactivated;
relax when overactivated;
notice approaching depletion;
distribute load;
recover.
Critical distinction from Interoception
Interoception is broad internal sensing.
Energy Regulation is specifically resource regulation.
Weak Interoception may constrain Energy Regulation because one cannot regulate signals one does not detect well, but they remain separate stats.
Diagnostic challenge
A proposed morning prediction vs evening reality test is useful for Layer C, but cannot serve as the sole Layer B test because it requires time.
Layer B needs momentary regulation tasks.
Candidate direction:
establish a baseline activation/tension state;
ask participant to intentionally down-regulate;
measure change;
optionally ask participant to intentionally mobilize afterward;
compare ability to shift state in both directions.
Potential measurements:
heart rate;
breathing rate;
subjective tension;
relaxation latency;
future HRV;
future respiration/camera sensing.
The exact standardized Self-Guided protocol remains unfinished.
STAT 3 --- INTEROCEPTION
Meaning
Sensitivity to, differentiation of and mapping of internal bodily signals.
Examples:
heartbeat;
breathing;
gastrointestinal signals;
hunger/satiety;
tension;
relaxation;
internal temperature;
organ sensations;
muscular states;
other internal signals.
It is broader than resource regulation.
Diagnostic direction
Body scan:
identify strongest tension/discomfort;
if no tension is reported, identify areas of greatest relaxation;
map location and type of sensation.
An important observed pattern: some visibly tense participants report "nothing," indicating low access/differentiation.
Heartbeat task
A heartbeat estimation/detection task was proposed but needs careful redesign.
The phrase "guess your heartbeats" is insufficient. A valid implementation needs a clear protocol and a comparison against an actual pulse measurement or validated interoceptive task. Do not use a naive heartbeat-counting score as scientific truth without validation.
Layer C
Especially valuable because NeuroAttention repeatedly asks:
what do you feel?
what type of sensation?
where is it?
how did it change?
Growth can be observed as increasing:
differentiation;
spatial precision;
vocabulary;
consistency;
number of detectable internal categories.
Objective markers
Direct "objective" validation of a reported tension location is difficult without sensors. Future options may include physiological sensing, but this remains open.
STAT 4 --- PROPRIOCEPTION
Meaning
Perception and control of the body in space and time.
Includes:
coordination;
movement accuracy;
balance;
body-position sense;
rhythm;
timing.
Guided test concept
Participant stands at a known point, closes eyes and marches with high knees while maintaining a rhythm and attempting to stay in place.
Measure:
displacement;
direction of drift;
rhythm stability;
coordination.
Guided mode is safer because the specialist can stop the person before collision.
Self-Guided alternatives
Safer candidates:
single-leg stance;
single-leg stance with controlled head turns;
eye movements while balancing;
touch-count / foot contacts;
finger-to-nose test;
coordination sequences.
Exact scoring norms remain to be validated.
STAT 5 --- VITAL ENGAGEMENT
Meaning
Capacity for interest, involvement, aliveness and contact with life as a process.
Not identical to:
happiness;
pleasure;
motivation.
Closer to:
"playing life";
felt richness;
genuine interest;
being drawn into processes.
Questionnaire direction
Frequency of genuine interest/aliveness over the last week, with behaviorally anchored answer ranges.
Behavioral challenge
A novelty/engagement challenge was proposed:
new route;
new action;
unfamiliar micro-practice;
then evaluate avoidance, engagement and absorption.
Problem: difficult to measure reliably inside a one-session Self-Guided Layer B diagnostic.
Therefore this stat is expected to rely more heavily on:
questionnaire;
immediate choice/engagement tasks;
Layer C longitudinal data.
Levels
A full 0--100 banding was intended but the exact canonical wording is not recoverable here. It must be reconstructed and validated rather than invented.
9. MIND CONTROL --- CONSOLIDATED SPECIFICATION
STAT 6 --- ATTENTION CONTROL
Meaning
Ability to voluntarily:
direct attention;
sustain attention;
notice distraction;
return attention;
switch attention intentionally.
Not the same as Awareness.
Core distinction
Attention Control asks:
Where is my attention, and can I control where it goes?
Awareness asks:
What is happening in the moment/process?
Levels
0--20 --- Scattered: attention chaotic, frequent unrecognized drift.
20--40 --- Reactive: short retention; external stimuli easily capture attention.
40--60 --- Functional: can sustain task attention, periodically distracts and returns.
60--80 --- Directed: intentionally directs and switches attention with limited loss.
80--100 --- Mastered: high voluntary control; low dependence on random stimuli.
Diagnostic questions
Three areas were proposed:
frequency of distraction during important tasks;
ability/speed of return after noticing distraction;
ease of sustaining one object for ~5 minutes.
Final wording should avoid obvious "good answer" bias.
Focus Retention Test
Digital sustained-attention task.
Example:
stream of symbols;
respond only to a target symbol.
Measure:
errors;
omissions;
reaction time;
performance drift over time.
Attention Disruption / Controlled Distraction Test
The earlier vague "measure productivity drop" was corrected.
Preferred implementation:
same attention task across phases;
phase 1: low distraction;
phase 2: distractors introduced;
phase 3: greater distraction;
compare accuracy/reaction metrics.
Derived candidate metric:
Attention Disruption Index.
This is automatable in Self-Guided mode.
NeuroAttention-specific alternative
Short breath-attention practice with controlled distractors and explicit markers for:
"noticed distraction";
"returned."
This may better reflect the actual skill trained by NeuroAttention than generic cognitive testing.
Layer C evidence
uninterrupted practice duration;
pauses;
completion;
return after distraction;
continuous work duration;
potentially tab/context switching if technically and ethically available.
Hidden/candidate metric
Attention Endurance --- how long quality attention can be sustained.
XP source categories
Do not lock numbers yet.
Potential XP sources:
sustained attention;
successful return after distraction;
completion of practices;
reduced distraction over time;
longer continuous concentration.
STAT 7 --- AWARENESS
Revised meaning
This stat was significantly corrected during discussion.
Preferred definition:
Ability to notice, distinguish and become aware of processes occurring within oneself and around oneself as they arise and unfold in real time.
Awareness is not merely intellectual understanding or retrospective analysis.
Key distinction
Knowledge: I know.
Understanding: I can explain.
Awareness: I notice the process while it is happening.
Example:
Interoception: "I feel anxiety."
Attention Control: "I can keep attention on the anxiety."
Awareness: "I notice anxiety arising after this interaction, being amplified by these thoughts, and affecting my choices right now."
Saying "I procrastinate a lot" is not automatically high Awareness. It may simply be retrospective knowledge.
Levels
0--20 --- Blind: most processes run automatically; causes rarely noticed.
20--40 --- Reactive Awareness: events mostly noticed after they occur.
40--60 --- Functional Awareness: notices some states/patterns and some causal relations.
60--80 --- Systemic Awareness: sees recurring relations among states, actions, environment and results; often notices processes during development.
80--100 --- Meta-Awareness: notices automatic reactions, attentional shifts, emotional processes and emerging intentions near the moment they begin, before they fully capture behavior.
Measurement problem
Awareness is poorly measured by "correct answers."
The diagnostic should measure:
number of processes noticed;
diversity of processes;
temporal sequencing;
ability to notice causal relationships;
real-time vs retrospective noticing.
Pattern Detection Test
Short life scenarios with multiple causal elements.
Useful, but it mainly measures pattern detection / causal interpretation, not full personal Awareness.
Therefore it should not dominate the stat.
State Mapping Test --- preferred Self-Guided task
After 3--5 minutes of breath observation, participant maps what occurred.
Candidate categories:
thoughts;
emotions;
bodily sensations;
breathing;
tension;
internal dialogue;
movement impulses;
external stimuli;
sleepiness;
interest;
anxiety;
boredom.
Then ask:
what appeared first?
what followed?
what intensified something else?
what reduced it?
what seemed primary?
Candidate indices:
Diversity Index
Process Awareness Index
Pattern Awareness Index
Layer C
Expected to be the main measurement source.
Analyze longitudinal notes/maps for:
depth;
number of factors;
temporal resolution;
causal links;
prediction;
consistency.
Candidate hidden metric
Pattern Resolution --- ability to notice, explain and predict patterns.
This may connect strongly to World Model Coherence.
XP source categories
insight;
stable pattern discovery;
successful prediction.
Again, exact XP amounts are not finalized.
STAT 8 --- ADAPTATION FLEXIBILITY
Meaning
Capacity to change existing patterns of:
behavior;
thinking;
attention;
reaction;
strategy;
identity/self-organization
when conditions or evidence require it.
A useful formulation from the discussion:
ability to let go of the previous self/pattern and allow a new one to emerge.
It is related to neuroplasticity, psychological flexibility and personality plasticity, but should not be casually equated with any single established construct without validation.
Relationship to Awareness
They are strongly coupled but should remain separate.
Awareness makes change possible by exposing a process in real time.
Adaptation Flexibility is the capacity to actually reorganize the pattern.
Important nuance: high Awareness with very low Flexibility should probably be less common than the earlier simplistic examples implied. The dependency should be reflected in the dependency model rather than by collapsing the stats.
Questionnaire design correction
Early items were rejected as too transparent, e.g.:
"I keep doing the same thing"
"I quickly adapt"
because users can see which answer sounds "good."
Questions must use non-moralized, behaviorally plausible alternatives.
Example direction:
"When facing a task you have already tried to solve..."
continue developing the current approach;
stay with it for a while, then look for alternatives;
begin testing another approach relatively quickly;
test several approaches in parallel.
No answer should scream "best person."
Rule Shift Test
Digital task where response rules change.
Measures:
cognitive flexibility;
errors after rule change;
adaptation latency.
Important correction:
This measures only part of Adaptation Flexibility, not the full construct.
Adaptation Scenario Test
Life scenarios such as:
new habit disrupted after several days;
feedback contradicts existing plan;
strategy stops working;
environmental change invalidates routine.
Measure response pattern without obvious moral hierarchy.
Behavioral Micro-Adaptation Challenge
Within the diagnostic, participant follows one rule/strategy and then receives controlled changes.
Still primarily a short-term proxy.
Layer C
Expected to be the strongest evidence:
response to new practices;
response to feedback;
recovery after lapses;
changing ineffective strategies;
adoption and stabilization of new behavior.
Candidate hidden metric
Adaptation Latency --- time between "I recognized the need for change" and "I actually changed behavior/strategy."
XP source categories
implemented change;
sustained new pattern;
successful return after disruption;
change under meaningful resistance.
Exact "Resistance XP" formula is unfinished.
STAT 9 --- EXECUTION
Meaning
Ability to turn intentions, decisions and plans into completed actions and real-world outcomes.
Not identical to:
motivation;
discipline;
busyness;
productivity.
Execution includes:
initiation;
continuation;
completion;
outcome production.
Distinction within Mind Control
Attention Control: I can hold/direct attention.
Awareness: I notice what is happening.
Adaptation Flexibility: I can reorganize/change.
Execution: I actually do and complete.
Levels
0--20 --- Inactive: many intentions, few completions.
20--40 --- Starter: starts but has difficulty finishing.
40--60 --- Functional: completes a meaningful portion of tasks.
60--80 --- Reliable: regularly finishes what is started; high behavioral predictability.
80--100 --- Integrator: systematically converts intentions into complex long-term results.
Diagnostic questions
Proposed domains:
transition from preparation to action;
persistence across multi-week tasks;
actual completion ratio over recent period.
Final wording still needs anti-social-desirability design.
Multi-Step Completion Test
Participant receives a sequence of linked steps.
Measure:
completed steps;
errors;
abandonment;
total time;
continuity.
Delayed Completion Test
An instruction/intention is planted early in the diagnostic and must be completed later.
This attempts to measure maintenance of an intended action across intervening tasks.
Caution: it also loads prospective memory, so it should not be interpreted as pure Execution.
Layer C
One of the strongest longitudinally measurable stats.
Possible evidence:
completed vs started practices;
abandoned tasks;
returns;
streaks;
long-cycle completion;
actual behavioral commitments.
Candidate hidden metrics
Completion Ratio = completed processes / initiated processes.
Intention-to-Action Gap = discrepancy between stated intention and actual behavior.
XP principle
Execution XP should emphasize completion, not mere initiation.
Exact values remain unfinished.
10. WORLD LAYER --- CONSOLIDATED SPECIFICATION
STAT 10 --- TECHNOPERCEPTION
Major conceptual correction
An earlier version defined Technoperception mainly as "conscious control of digital environment." That was rejected.
That construct is closer to:
Technoperception × Awareness → Tech Awareness.
Technoperception itself includes technological competence.
Meaning
Ability to perceive, understand, learn and use technologies as a domain/environment of interaction.
Includes:
digital literacy;
technological literacy;
ease of learning tools;
ability to use digital systems;
technological problem-solving;
comfort with new interfaces;
ability to combine tools.
A person may have high Technoperception and low Awareness, e.g. technically brilliant but poorly aware of how technology affects their attention/life.
Levels
0--20 --- Digitally Disconnected: significant difficulty learning/using technology.
20--40 --- Basic User: uses basic tools; learns new systems mostly when necessary.
40--60 --- Functional User: confidently uses common digital tools.
60--80 --- Advanced User: quickly learns new technologies and environments.
80--100 --- Technology Native: technological environments feel like a natural extension of activity; combines tools to solve complex problems.
Diagnostic questions
Domains:
response to unfamiliar digital tools;
solving new tasks through technology;
comfort in unfamiliar apps/platforms/AI tools/operating systems.
Again, wording must avoid obvious "high-tech = morally better" bias.
Tool Discovery Test
Give a participant a novel mini-interface.
Tasks:
find functions;
execute actions;
navigate without prior instruction.
Measure:
learning speed;
errors;
hints required;
exploration strategy.
Digital Problem Solving Test
Provide a task plus multiple tools/options.
Measure effectiveness in selecting and applying technology.
Layer C
Potential evidence:
adoption of platform functions;
speed of learning new features;
variety of tools;
successful integration into workflows.
Candidate hidden metric
Technology Adaptability --- speed/quality of learning a new technological environment.
Intersection with Socioperception
Social media, online reputation, public digital presence etc. are hybrid zones:
Technoperception × Socioperception
Technoperception concerns use of the technological medium.
Socioperception concerns the social body expressed through that medium.
STAT 11 --- SOCIOPERCEPTION
Meaning
Ability to perceive, understand and interact with one's social body.
The "social body" includes:
activity/work;
projects;
business;
mission;
contribution;
reputation;
social role;
place in society;
place in team/community;
public manifestation;
physical and digital social presence.
Key distinction
Socioperception is not simply "social skills."
It concerns:
I ↔ social system
Questions such as:
What do I create?
What is my role?
Who needs it?
What value do I contribute?
Where do I stand in the social ecosystem?
Physical + digital social body
Social body exists in both:
physical space;
digital space.
Therefore possible manifestations include:
professional reputation;
communities;
public presence;
digital identity;
social networks;
content;
public projects;
educational/social activity.
A person may have very high Socioperception without maintaining social-media accounts if their social body is strongly expressed physically/offline.
Levels
0--20 --- Socially Disconnected: weak understanding of role/place/contribution.
20--40 --- Searching: searching for social identity/direction.
40--60 --- Functional: understands core activity and roles.
60--80 --- Social Contributor: understands value and actively contributes to projects/communities.
80--100 --- Social Architect: creates projects, communities, organizations or new social structures.
Diagnostic questions
Domains:
ability to describe one's activity;
understanding who benefits from it;
role in groups/teams/communities.
Social Mapping Test
Map:
projects;
work;
clients;
communities;
teams;
audience;
partners;
social networks/public platforms.
Measure:
Social Structure Index --- clarity/completeness of one's social-body map.
Value Flow Test
Questions:
What do you create?
For whom?
What do they receive?
What do you receive?
Candidate metric:
Social Value Awareness / Social Value Clarity
(Name should be reviewed because "Awareness" is already a separate stat.)
Guided version
With consent, specialist may inspect:
public profiles;
social networks;
projects;
professional activity;
communities.

Layer C
Possible evidence:
contribution to community;
initiatives;
projects;
collaboration;
public/educational activity.
Candidate hidden metric
Social Role Clarity --- clarity of role, function, contribution and value in social systems.
STAT 12 --- RELATIONAL AWARENESS
Major conceptual refinement
Earlier description "person ↔ person" was expanded.
Preferred core:
transition from "I" to "We."
Relational Awareness is the capacity to create and participate in a shared relational system.
"We" can be:
couple;
family;
friendship;
team;
group;
close community.
Meaning
Ability to:
notice relational dynamics;
understand others;
create connection;
maintain connection;
deepen connection;
collaborate;
build trust;
repair damaged relationships.
Charisma / extraversion / sociability
Earlier hard exclusion ("not charisma, not sociability, not number of friends") was corrected.
Preferred formulation:
Relational Awareness does not reduce to charisma, extraversion, sociability or number of friends, but these can be correlated indicators or contributing traits.
Examples:
charisma can facilitate initial connection;
sociability can facilitate contact formation;
many stable friendships may be evidence of relationship-maintenance capacity.
But none guarantees relational quality.
Distinction from Socioperception
Socioperception:
I ↔ social system
role, contribution, mission, social manifestation.
Relational Awareness:
I ↔ We
trust, connection, closeness, cooperation, shared relational space.
Levels --- earlier working version
0--20 --- Relationally Isolated: difficulty creating/sustaining stable relationships.
20--40 --- Transactional: relationships often remain functional/transactional.
40--60 --- Functional: maintains stable relationships and basic understanding of others.
60--80 --- Connected: creates deep, stable relationships and sees relational dynamics.
80--100 --- Relational Integrator: creates conditions of trust, cooperation and development across people/groups.
These labels are provisional and should be reviewed.
Diagnostic questions
Domains:
response to another person's difficult state;
behavior after conflict;
maintenance/development of important relationships.
Questions need anti-social-desirability rewriting.
Social Scenario Test
Interpret ambiguous interpersonal situations.
Measure ability to hold multiple plausible interpretations rather than instantly projecting one story.
Candidate metric:
Relational Interpretation Index.
Perspective Shift Test
Describe/interpret a situation from:
own position;
another participant's position.
Candidate metric:
Perspective Flexibility.
Layer C
Potential evidence:
cooperation;
peer feedback;
conflict repair;
contribution to group processes;
relationship maintenance.
This requires strong privacy/consent design.
Candidate hidden metric
Initial proposal: Trust Capacity.
Refined proposal: Relationship Capacity --- ability to create, sustain, deepen and repair relationships.

11. STAT INTERDEPENDENCE
A major architectural decision:
Stats are not independent sliders.
Examples:
weak Interoception can constrain Energy Regulation;
Space Awareness can affect Energy Regulation and Attention Control through environmental load;
Technoperception can affect Attention Control, Energy Regulation, Vital Engagement and Execution;
Awareness strongly supports Adaptation Flexibility;
relationships affect Stability, Energy Regulation and Vital Engagement.
The intended artifact is a 13 × 13 NeuroAttention Dependency Matrix.
Each directed relationship should specify:
source stat;
target stat;
strength;
type.
Types previously used conceptually:
Foundation
Amplifier
Constraint
0 / negligible
A prior incomplete Body-Core-centric matrix was rejected because it did not cover all 13 stats.
A later full 13-stat matrix was reportedly discussed/accepted, but the exact 13×13 numeric values are not recoverable in the available context. They must be retrieved from the original source if available or reconstructed with explicit review rather than fabricated.
Important scoring caution
The idea "low Interoception = automatically subtract 10 XP from Energy Regulation" was raised as an example, not finalized.
Better future architecture likely separates:
measured score of stat A;
confidence in stat B;
growth ceiling/rate modifier;
dependency penalty/modifier.
Do not casually change an observed stat score solely because another stat is low without a defensible model.

Better current abstraction: XP SOURCES
For each stat, identify what types of behavior can generate evidence/progression.
Examples:
Stability - maintaining function under load; - recovery; - consistency through long practice.
Attention Control - sustained attention; - returning after distraction; - longer uninterrupted focus.
Awareness - detecting new patterns; - higher-resolution state mapping; - validated prediction.

Execution - completing rather than merely starting.
Technoperception - learning and successfully applying a new tool.
Socioperception - contribution and creation of social value.
Relational Awareness - relationship creation, maintenance, deepening, repair.

15. NORMATIVE GROWTH MODEL
A major accepted principle:
Real human capacities should not jump unrealistically.
Example explicitly rejected:
Stability 30 → 80 in one month.
The intended time horizons are:
1 month;
3 months;
6 months;
12 months;
potentially multi-year.
The core NeuroAttention course was around 3 months at the time of discussion, with a likely move toward 6 months and ongoing subscription/tool access afterward.
Some earlier stat-specific growth ranges were drafted (especially Mind Control / World stats), but they were heuristic, not empirically validated.
Examples of previously proposed rough ranges included larger early Awareness gains and slower Adaptation/Execution/relationship gains.
Do not treat these as scientific norms.
Next-stage requirement:
Build a normative model using:
actual participant baseline/retest data;
measurement reliability;
expected practice dose;
adherence;
guided vs self-guided condition;
regression to mean;
ceiling effects;
minimal detectable change;
eventually population percentiles.

19. TEST DESIGN REQUIREMENTS
Avoid social-desirability traps
A major issue was identified in early questionnaire wording.
Bad item:
"When my strategy fails, I keep doing the same thing / I quickly adapt."
The "correct" answer is obvious.
Preferred design:
behaviorally anchored;
morally neutral;
plausible tradeoffs;
forced choice where useful;
scenario-based;
no "hero answer."
Triangulate evidence
Do not derive a stat from one question.
Use combinations of:
questionnaire;
behavioral task;
course telemetry;
guided observation;
future sensors.
Separate construct contamination
Many tests measure multiple things.
Examples:
delayed completion also measures prospective memory;
rule shifting measures cognitive flexibility, not whole-person plasticity;
memory under physical load is partly memory;
social scenarios can measure verbal intelligence.
Every test should declare:
primary construct;
secondary constructs;
confounds.
Repeatability
Layer C requires alternate forms / repeated measures to reduce practice effects.
Safety
Physical tests need:
contraindication screening;
safe alternatives;
clear stop rules;
non-exercise version where needed.

25. IMPORTANT DESIGN DECISIONS TO PRESERVE
Do not collapse Interoception into Energy Regulation.
Do not define Awareness as retrospective intellectual understanding.
Do not define Technoperception only as conscious digital hygiene.
Do not collapse Socioperception into interpersonal relationships.
Relational Awareness is fundamentally "I → We," not simply popularity.
Charisma/sociability can be indicators, but not the whole Relational stat.
Digital social presence is a Socioperception × Technoperception hybrid zone.
Stats are interdependent.
Layer B is a baseline model; Layer C is longitudinal refinement.
Some stats deserve lower initial confidence than others.
Do not finalize XP numbers before scoring/evidence architecture.
Do not allow unrealistic stat growth merely because a user completed content.
Course completion is evidence, not automatically development.
Long practices and the manner of completing them are themselves behavioral data.
Profiles should evolve because the underlying configuration changes.
World Model Coherence remains separate from the 13 percentage stats.
Core Power was not accepted as a replacement for character progression.
The final system must be capable of future biometric/neurofeedback integration without requiring it for the current MVP.
